FinderGit 0.72.0

Release notes

FinderGit 0.72.0 — Root folders that follow a move, and groups a click away

Highlights

  • A root folder you move or rename comes along. FinderGit kept the full path of each root, so moving or renaming the folder left a root that showed nothing, and the only way back was removing it and adding it again. Now the next scan (at launch, or ↻) finds the folder in its new place on the same disk, and keeps its group, its position in the list, and the Favorites and Recents inside it. A folder you had drilled into stays open where it went.
  • A root FinderGit can't find says so. One that was deleted, copied to another disk, or sits on a drive that isn't connected shows a question mark in the sidebar, and selecting it says where FinderGit looked. Right-click it → Change Location… to show FinderGit where it is now: it keeps its group and its place in the list.
  • Click a group's name to open or close it, as its arrow does. The name used to do nothing.
  • File → New Group… creates a group in Root Folders, as Add Group… in the sidebar's + menu does. Creating a group also opens Root Folders when the section is collapsed, so the new group is never out of sight.

Under the hood

  • Each root keeps a bookmark beside its path, carrying the folder's file identifier. A scan resolves the bookmark of a root that is no longer at its path, without mounting a volume or asking anything, and refuses a target that isn't a folder or is in the Trash. A different folder later created at the old path is recognised as different: it gets a bookmark of its own and is never taken for the one that moved.
  • Roots nested inside a moved root move with it, deepest first. Favorites, Recents and the window's Back and Forward history follow by path prefix, so moving /a/foo leaves /a/foobar alone.

Install

  1. Download FinderGit-0.72.0.dmg
  2. Open the DMG and drag FinderGit into Applications (or open it from there, and it offers to move itself)
  3. Launch normally — the app is signed with an Apple Developer ID and notarized by Apple

Requirements

macOS 15 or later. Universal binary (Apple Silicon and Intel).
