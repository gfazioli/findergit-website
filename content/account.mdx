# Account

The **Account** view turns FinderGit into a window onto your GitHub profile — avatar, bio, the numbers that matter, and a full year of contributions — without leaving the app or opening a browser.

![The Account dashboard — profile header, stat tiles, and the contribution graph](/screenshot-hero-account.png)

## Your profile at a glance

The header pulls your GitHub identity: avatar, name, `@login`, bio, company, location, website, and the year you joined — with a button straight to your profile page.

Beside the page title sit **Clone from GitHub…** and a refresh button, as on the [Overview](/docs/overview). On macOS 27 you can also pull the page down to refresh your profile, as in Mail: the spinner holds for a moment and lets go, and the figures fill in as the answers arrive.

## The numbers

A grid of stat tiles covering both your social proof and your activity:

- **Followers / Following**
- **Stars** — summed across your repositories
- **Public Repos / Private Repos** — your own non-fork repositories
- **Forks** — how many of your repos are forks
- **Pull Requests** (with how many merged) and **Issues** opened
- **Commits** in the last year, and the number of repositories you **contributed** to
- **Organizations** you belong to

The tiles land one after another as the page opens, their figures rolling up from zero, and roll again when you refresh.

The cards are grouped by subject: people (Followers, Following, Organizations), repositories (Public and Private Repos, Forks, Languages), stars (Stars, Top repositories) and activity (Commits, Contributed, Contributions, Pull Requests, Issues). Five of them open: **Organizations**, **Languages**, **Stars**, **Top repositories** and **Contributions**. Click one and it grows into its panel right under its row; one is open at a time, a second click closes it, and FinderGit remembers which was open. **Contributions** is open the first time.

<Callout type="info" emoji="🔒">
Because FinderGit runs entirely on your Mac with your own GitHub sign-in, it can show your **private** numbers too — private repo counts and the like — that a public profile page hides.
</Callout>

## Share it as a Profile Card

Click **Profile Card** at the top right of the page to see the whole page as one picture made to be shared, with your repositories' figures counted from public repositories only, in nine looks. Save it as an image or a PDF, copy it, or share it: see [Profile Card](/docs/profile-card).

## Contributions, two ways

The **Contributions** card opens a full 52-week contribution calendar, with month and weekday labels just like GitHub. The toggle in the panel's top-right switches between the classic **heatmap grid** and a **line graph** of daily contributions over the year, and FinderGit keeps your choice:

![The contribution panel switched to the line-graph view](/screenshot-account-graph.png)

## Top repositories & languages, as charts

**Top repositories** opens your most-starred repositories, and **Languages** a breakdown of the languages you work in. Each panel has a switch at its top right between a **donut**, **bars** and the plain **list**, and FinderGit remembers the choice for each panel. The donut (the default) sets every entry against the whole and folds the rest into one grey **Other**, with the total in the middle; hover a name and its slice lights up. In Top repositories a click opens the repository on GitHub. The same switch is on the [Overview](/docs/overview#top-starred--largest-on-disk)'s two rankings.

![Languages as a donut: each language a slice in its own colour, the rest folded into Other, the number of repositories in the middle](/screenshot-account-languages.png)

## Organizations

**Organizations** is a card that opens: one row for each organization you belong to, with its name and its login, and a click takes you to its page on GitHub. The card counts every membership; the panel lists the ones GitHub returns for display.

## New-star notifications

FinderGit can keep an eye on your repositories while it's running and let you know the moment one earns a star — naming the repository, not just bumping a number.

- A green **+** appears beside the chevron of the **Stars** card whenever a repo gains a star. Open the card and its panel lists exactly which repositories were starred, with how many and when; click a row to open that repository on GitHub. Opening the card marks the stars as seen.
- Optionally, enable **desktop notifications** in **Settings → Git → Star Notifications** (off by default) and pick how often to check (every 10 / 30 / 60 minutes). The in-app badge works either way — turning notifications off doesn't hide your new stars.

![The Recent stars list the Stars card opens into: which repositories gained stars, how many, and when](/screenshot-recent-stars.png)

<Callout type="info" emoji="💡">
The marker and notifications share one baseline, so you're never told about the same star twice — and a relaunch won't re-announce stars earned while the app was closed.
</Callout>
